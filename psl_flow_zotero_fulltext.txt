PSL-Flow: Physics-Structured Latent Flow Matching
for Aerial Visible-to-Infrared Image Translation
Leping Lin, Zibin Zheng, and Ning Ouyang
Abstract—Aerial visible-to-infrared (V2IR) image translation provides scalable data support for nighttime remote sensing. However, existing methods predominantly learn cross-modal mappings within pixel or generic latent spaces, where physical information acts merely as an auxiliary training constraint. This limitation hinders physical cues from continuously participating in thermal structure generation during inference. In this letter, we propose PSL-Flow, a novel framework for aerial V2IR image generation via conditional flow matching within a physicsstructured latent space. PSL-Flow shifts the generative objective from direct pixel mapping to physical quantity distribution modeling, enabling physical information to serve as intrinsic generative variables. Specifically, physical quantities including temperature are estimated from real infrared images to construct an intermediate representation endowed with thermal imaging semantics. Subsequently, the physical backbone and local residuals are jointly encoded into a physics-structured latent space, transforming the objective from pixel mapping to thermal state modeling. Ultimately, a continuous generation process driven by visible conditions is learned within this latent space, and infrared images are reconstructed through the synergistic decoding of the physical backbone and boundary residuals. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves stable advantages across multiple aerial datasets.
Index Terms—Aerial visible-to-infrared image translation, physics-structured latent space, flow matching, thermal state modeling
I. INTRODUCTION
ERIAL infrared (IR) imagery is essential for allweather remote sensing because it provides a stable characterization of thermal radiation. However, the acquisition of paired visible-to-infrared (VIS-IR) samples depends on expensive sensors and precise registration, which hinders large-scale applications. Consequently, aerial visibleto-infrared (V2IR) translation and its associated tasks have become a research direction with clear practical needs [1]-[3]. With the development of generative models [6], approaches such as Brownian bridge diffusion [7] and ThermalGen [8] introduced powerful tools including latent diffusion models [9] and scalable interpolant Transformers (SiT) [10]. Although these methods possess efficient distribution transport capabilities, such cross-modal mappings are inherently learned within generic pixel or feature spaces. As a result, they struggle to explicitly characterize the formation of thermal structures, as infrared thermal properties are not formulated as sampleable state variables. To bridge the gap between visual similarity and thermal plausibility, recent studies have introduced physical modeling into infrared generation. Frameworks such as PID [11] and IPLD [12] inject physical constraints through thermodynamic decomposition, demonstrating the positive impact of physical priors on thermal representation. Indeed, explicit modeling of physical information significantly alleviates the ill-posed nature of V2IR mapping. However, in these methods, physical decomposition serves merely as supervision during training or as a conditional enhancement. During the inference process, reverse sampling still originates from generic noise, which means physical information does not continuously participate in the actual generative trajectory. To address this limitation, we propose PSL-Flow, an aerial V2IR framework that shifts the generative objective from direct infrared image regression to the modeling of physical quantity distributions. Specifically, we utilize a TeR-B module to estimate temperature (T), emissivity (e), environmental radiance ( env
R ), and thermal boundary responses (B) from real infrared images. These factors are then organized into a physics-structured latent space via a PSL-VAE. Finally, SiT is employed to learn a continuous flow-matching process driven by visible light conditions within this latent space [6], [10]. Crucially, by sampling explicitly organized thermal states during the inference stage, PSL-Flow ensures that physical information remains intrinsic to the generative trajectory. The main contributions are as follows: 1) We propose the TeR-B guided PSL-VAE, which transforms infrared image generation from pixel reconstruction to multi-factor physical thermal state modeling. 2) We introduce the PSL-Flow framework, which represents the first attempt to shift the generative objective from infrared image regression to physical quantity distribution modeling. 3) Experiments demonstrate the advantages of the proposed method regarding structural fidelity, perceptual quality, and physical consistency.

II. METHOD

R , and B to construct an intermediate representation endowed with explicit thermal semantics. Subsequently, the PSL-VAE jointly encodes these physical factors and local residuals into a physics-structured latent space, thereby transforming the generative objective from pixel mapping to thermal state modeling. Within this space, the SiT learns a continuous-time vector field that maps a Gaussian prior to the target thermal state. This approach fundamentally redefines the V2IR translation paradigm by ensuring that physical information functions as the underlying organizational mechanism during the inference stage.

During the inference stage, the generative process originates from pure Gaussian noise. An ordinary differential equation (ODE) solver within the trained SiT dynamically samples the predicted physical latent states. The PSL-VAE decoder then maps these states back to the physical backbone and boundary-modulated residuals to synergistically reconstruct the final infrared image. III. EXPERIMENT RESULTS AND ANALYSIS
A. Experimental Setup
The proposed PSL-Flow is evaluated on the AVIID [14], CART [15], and DRONEVEHICLE [16] datasets. To ensure spatial alignment, all images are resized to 256 256 . Performance is quantified using PSNR, SSIM [17], LPIPS [18], and FID [19]. The model is implemented via PyTorch 2.0.1 and trained on an NVIDIA Tesla A100 GPU using the AdamW optimizer with a batch size of 16. During the inference stage, we employ dopri5 as the ODE solver with a sampling budget of 50 steps. Baseline comparisons include StegoGAN [17], IRFormer [5], DR-AVIT [4], USTNet [1], and PID [11].

IV. CONCLUSION
This letter proposes a PSL-Flow, which reformulates aerial V2IR translation from direct image synthesis into explicit thermal state modeling. By executing visible-conditioned flow matching within a physics-structured latent space, the framework fundamentally elevates physical guidance from a training-time supervision signal to an active sampling state during inference, offering a new paradigm for infrared image generation. Extensive experiments across multiple datasets confirm that this paradigm significantly enhances structural fidelity, perceptual quality, and physical consistency compared with existing methods. Future work will explore more advanced distribution alignment strategies and direct physical validation to further investigate this generative paradigm based on physical sampling.
